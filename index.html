    How the descriptors are assigned

    Each benzene ring is a chiral plane. Following Strutt, Zhang, Schneebeli and Stoddart (Chem. Eur. J. 2014), the pilot atom is the first carbon of the neighbouring unit (always on the cavity side); 1 = the CH₂ bonded to it, 2 = the ring carbon bonded to that CH₂, 3 = the adjacent ring carbon bearing O. Viewed from the pilot, 1→2→3 clockwise = pR, anticlockwise = pS. The labels are computed live from the displayed 3D coordinates. The assembled model is all-pR by this convention; the correspondence to experimentally determined absolute configurations has not been verified — use the A/B labels if you prefer a neutral description.

    Symmetry classes use the idealised Dn skeleton: P[5] has 8 distinct orientation patterns (4 enantiomeric pairs, none achiral); P[6] has 13 (5 enantiomeric pairs and 3 achiral patterns). Mechanical warnings come from the CAD collision sweep of the printed kit; they are not rotational energy barriers.
